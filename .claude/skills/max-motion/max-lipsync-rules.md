# Max Lip Sync Rules

This file defines how Max's mouth and facial timing should behave during speech.

## Core principle

Lip sync must support:
- clarity
- emotion
- rhythm
- natural speaking behavior

It must not look:
- overanimated
- mechanical
- nonstop
- disconnected from expression

## General rules

- mouth does not move every frame
- include closed-mouth holds
- include transition shapes
- stressed syllables read more clearly
- vowels open more fully than weak syllables
- consonant closures are readable
- emotion affects mouth intensity

## Timing rules

- hold key mouth accents slightly longer
- allow micro-rests between phrases
- during strong body actions, simplify mouth motion
- during calm speech, mouth can be more readable
- do not let lip sync outrun the face acting

## Face coordination

Speech should coordinate with:
- eye focus
- brows
- cheeks
- blink timing
- jaw energy
- head movement

The face should never feel like:
"only the mouth is animated."

## Emotional lip sync intensity

### Calm
- smaller mouth range
- softer transitions

### Friendly
- clear smile support
- open readable shapes

### Excited
- quicker accents
- brighter mouth energy
- stronger openings

### Reassuring
- smoother transitions
- less sharp emphasis
- calmer jaw movement

## Anti-robot checklist

Before finalizing, check:
- is the mouth moving too much?
- are there closed-mouth rests?
- does the mouth match the emotion?
- does the face support the line?
- do blinks and eyes feel alive?
- does the line feel spoken rather than spelled out?
